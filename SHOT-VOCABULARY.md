# Shot vocabulary

Choose one camera movement and one framing decision. These terms are planning language; a model may interpret them imperfectly.

| Movement | Plain meaning | Useful check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `static` | Camera position and direction stay fixed. | Does the background drift even though only the subject should move? |
| `slow-push-in` | Camera moves closer to the subject. | Does perspective change gently without the object stretching? |
| `slow-pull-back` | Camera moves away, revealing more context. | Is newly revealed space coherent? |
| `pan-left` / `pan-right` | Camera turns horizontally from a fixed position. | Did the scene turn, or did the model move the subject instead? |
| `tilt-up` / `tilt-down` | Camera turns vertically from a fixed position. | Does the object retain its proportions during the reveal? |
| `track-left` / `track-right` | Camera moves sideways rather than simply turning. | Does foreground/background motion agree with the intended movement? |
| `gentle-handheld` | Small irregular camera movement. | Is the movement useful, or does it obscure the subject? |

A zoom changes focal length; a push-in changes camera position. This kit does not treat them as synonyms. If your tool only supports a preset zoom effect, use its own terminology and record that limitation.

## Framing

| Framing | Intended information |
| --- | --- |
| Wide shot | Subject in relation to a place. |
| Medium shot | Subject and a visible action, with some surroundings. |
| Close-up | One subject detail, expression or material. |
| Overhead | A view from above; useful for layouts and simple demonstrations. |
| Low angle | A viewpoint below the subject; separate this from any movement. |

“Close-up” says how much is visible. “Static” says how the camera behaves. They can coexist. “Static camera while orbiting the product” requests conflicting motion unless it describes separate shots.

## Three questions before rendering

1. Which element moves: subject, environment, camera, or a deliberately simple combination?
2. What must remain recognizable at the start, middle and end?
3. What does this shot communicate that a still image would not?

Do not use camera terminology as decoration. Remove a movement that adds no useful information.
